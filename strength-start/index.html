Where To Start

A plan only has to decide three things: which movements, how heavy, and when to go up. Most lists only answer the first one. Here are all three, free, on one page.

1 — The Seven

Seven movements cover it. Not seventy.

  1. Squat
  2. Romanian deadlift
  3. Split squat, or a step-up
  4. Chest press
  5. Overhead press
  6. Row
  7. Carry

Five of the seven per session, two different sessions, two or three days a week. At home, with dumbbells and a chair.

2 — The Test

Everyone says lift heavy. Nobody says how heavy.

  • Test each movement on its own. Do ten reps.
  • The right weight starts to feel challenging somewhere around rep six to eight.
  • If all ten feel easy, go up and test again.
For the first two weeks, use little or no weight — on purpose. You are learning the movement, and the first weight is not the number that matters.

3 — The Rule

The part a list can't tell you: when to add weight.

When you finish all three sets of fifteen with good form and still have two or three reps left in you, go up one weight next session.

That's it. You write the reps down; the reps tell you when to move up.

Before you start. This is a starting plan, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before beginning any new training, and stop if a movement hurts — sore the next day is normal, sharp pain during a lift is not.
